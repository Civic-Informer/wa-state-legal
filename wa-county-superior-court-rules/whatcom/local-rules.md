## **ADMINISTRATIVE RULES (WCAR)** 

## **WCAR 0.1 – Purpose and Citation** 

- a) **Purpose** . Procedure in Whatcom County Superior Court shall comply with the Revised Code of Washington, Washington Court Rules and these Whatcom County Superior Court Local Rules. 

- b) **Citation** . These rules are collectively referred to as “Whatcom County Superior Court Local Rules” or “WCLR” and individually as designated herein. 

- [Effective 6/1/91, amended 9/1/24] 

## **WCAR 0.2 – Definitions** 

Rules applying to an “attorney,” “counsel” or “lawyer” shall also apply to unrepresented litigants (URLs). Unless otherwise required by law or court rule, the terms “judge” and “court” include court commissioners. The term “clerk” includes employees authorized to act on behalf of the Whatcom County Superior Court Clerk. 

- [Effective 6/1/91, amended 9/1/24] 

4 

## **WCAR 0.3 – Organization of the Court** 

- a) Each Whatcom County Superior Court Judge, including any water law judge, is assigned to a Department to which cases are assigned. Constitutional commissioners, statutory commissioner(s) (including water law commissioner(s)), water law referee(s), and _pro tem_ commissioners and/or judges also conduct the business of the court. 

- b) The judges shall elect a Presiding Judge as set forth in WCGR 29, whose duties are set forth in that rule. 

- c) Court commissioners shall have authority in all matters allowed by the Washington State Constitution, statutes and case law including but not limited to the authority noted in RCW 2.24.040 to accept guilty pleas. Statutory commissioners shall have authority as defined by applicable statutes. 

[Amended 9/1/17, 9/1/22, 9/1/23, 9/1/24] 

## **WCAR 0.4 – Suspension of Rules** 

A judicial officer may modify or suspend any WCLR for good cause or upon the court’s own motion in order to prevent a failure of justice. 

[Effective 6/1/91, amended 9/1/25] 

## **WCAR 0.5 – Unsuitable Materials as Exhibits** 

Whenever material is presented to the clerk for filing that is deemed by the clerk to be improper or inappropriate for filing, the clerk shall mark the exhibit for the file and may orally apply to the court for a determination of the propriety of filing the material. If the court determines that the material should not be part of the file, an order shall be entered to that effect and the clerk shall retain the material as an exhibit in the case. The court may order that the material be sealed, in which event it shall be available for inspection only by the court, the parties and their attorneys of record, or by further order of the court. 

In appeals from administrative hearings, records of the administrative proceedings and exhibits are presumed, upon designation by a party, to be exhibits in the court file. 

[Effective 9/1/17, amended 9/1/25]

## **WCGR 11 – Interpreters** 

In any judicial proceeding in which a sign language or foreign language interpreter is required, requests for a qualified interpreter should be emailed to SCinterpreter@co.whatcom.wa.us, at least five (5) court days prior to the hearing when possible, with the case name and number, the date and time of the hearing, and the type of interpreter needed. 

In criminal matters, the court will secure qualified interpreters for defendants appearing in custody for First Appearance and Arraignment. For defendants appearing out of custody for First Appearance and Arraignment, the Prosecuting Attorney’s Office must notify the court, at least five (5) court days prior to each hearing when possible, of the need for an interpreter by emailing the request to SCinterpreter@co.whatcom.wa.us with the case name and number, the date and time of the hearing, and the type of interpreter needed. 

For any subsequent criminal hearings, the defense must notify the court, at least five (5) court days prior to each hearing when possible, of the need for an interpreter by emailing the request to SCinterpreter@co.whatcom.wa.us with the case name and number, the date and time of the hearing, and the type of interpreter needed. 

Use of an interpreter in a judicial proceeding is subject to court approval. 

[Effective 9/1/23, amended 9/1/25] 

## **WCGR 16 – Recording Court Proceedings** 

No proceedings of the court, including remote or telephonic proceedings, may be recorded, by any means, unless the judicial officer conducting the proceedings has authorized the recording. Requests for approval should be made to the judicial assistant for the judicial officer scheduled to hear the case at least one judicial day prior to the scheduled hearing, if practicable. All recordings must be conducted as provided in Washington Court Rule GR 16. 

[Effective 9/1/20, amended 9/1/23, 9/1/24] 

## **WCGR 19 – Remote Proceedings** 

6 

- a) **“Remote Proceeding”** means a video and/or audio appearance in which all participants can simultaneously hear and be heard when authorized by the judicial officer. Remote appearances shall be deemed to be held in open court when in the presence of all other participants. 

- b) **“Participants”** means the parties, their legal representatives, the public, the clerk, the court reporter, any interpreter and the judicial officer. 

- c) Standards for remote proceedings. 

   - 1) **Decorum** . All participants are expected to follow court orders, rules and policies on appropriate courtroom decorum during remote appearances. The court retains the discretion to remove any participant for failure to follow court orders, including dress and decorum (see WCCR 77.1). 

   - 2) **Quality of video/audio** . In a remote proceeding, video and/or audio must be of sufficient quality to ensure that all participants can see (if applicable), hear and be heard when so permitted by the judicial officer. Filters, including backgrounds, are not allowed during video appearances. Participants must have head and shoulders fully visible in the video frame. If the judicial officer determines that the quality of the audio and/or video is insufficient, the judicial officer may continue the matter and require in-person attendance, or may refuse to consider any statements made remotely, or may order other relief in furtherance of justice. By prior request through a judicial assistant, a judicial officer may allow a witness to testify remotely, subject to the above requirements. 

- d) **Participation in remote proceedings** . Unless otherwise instructed by the court, participants are encouraged not to enable video in order to facilitate improved audio connections. All participants appearing remotely must mute their microphones unless specifically addressing the court. Unless otherwise ordered by the court, all remote appearances shall be open to the public. 

- [Effective 9/1/22, amended 9/1/25] 

## **WCGR 28 – Jury Service Postponement, Excusal and Disqualification** 

The jury coordinator and/or the judicial assistants are authorized to exercise discretion to disqualify, postpone, or excuse potential jurors from jury service during orientation and prior to the start of jury selection upon showing of undue hardship, extreme inconvenience, public necessity, failure to meet the criteria of RCW 2.36.070 or as authorized by a judge for another reason deemed sufficient. At the discretion of the jury coordinator and/or judicial assistant, or at 

7 

the discretion of a judge, a prospective juror excused from service may be assigned to another jury term within twelve (12) months. 

[Effective 9/1/25] 

## **WCGR 29 – Presiding Judge** 

The judges shall elect a Presiding Judge and an Assistant Presiding Judge by majority vote to serve a two-year term, starting on January 1 of odd-numbered years. Any ties will be broken by the current Presiding Judge. Presiding Judge will also act as a Special Inquiry Judge, unless a different judge is designated by Presiding Judge.  Presiding or Assistant Presiding Judge may be removed at any time by unanimous vote of the other judges or by resignation, at which time an election will be held to fill the remainder of the term. 

Presiding Judge shall assign the duties and trial responsibilities of each department, the commissioners and the referee(s). Presiding Judge may delegate duties to any other judge in the case of temporary absence. 

In assigning a judge to oversee any general water rights adjudication filed pursuant to RCW 90.03.140 and RCW 90.44.220, the Presiding Judge will consider the projected length of the adjudication, the experience of the judge, and will give particular weight to the importance of consistency through the period of the adjudication. At the Presiding Judge’s discretion, the decision to assign that judge may be delegated to a majority vote of the bench, with any ties broken by the Presiding Judge. 

[Effective 9/1/25] 

## **WCGR 30 – Electronic Filing and Service** 

**Application** This rule applies to all case types specifically authorized in the Whatcom County Local Rules for electronic filing. All provisions below apply solely to those designated case types. 

## (a) **Definitions.** 

## **[RESERVED]** 

## (b) **Electronic Filing** . 

(1) **Mandatory Electronic Filing** . Attorneys shall electronically file (eFile) all documents using the eFiling system unless this rule provides otherwise. Unrepresented litigants are not required to eFile but may do so. 

8 

(2) **Documents That Shall Not Be eFiled** . The following documents shall be filed in paper form and not eFiled: 

- (A) Original paper wills and codicils (excluding electronic wills), including will only and new probate cases that include original paper wills or codicils; 

- (B) Documents submitted for in-camera review pursuant to the relevant local rule and GR 15; 

- (C) New cases or fee-based documents filed with an Order to Waive Fees or in accordance with GR 34; 

- (D) New case filings when there is a request for immediate relief; 

- (E) Affidavits for Writs for Garnishment and Writs of Execution. 

- (F) Requests for accommodations pursuant to GR 33; 

- (G) Interpleader or Surplus Funds Petitions; 

- (H) Documents presented for filing during a court hearing or trial; 

- (I) Certified records of proceedings for purposes of appeal; 

- (J) Ex parte motions, as provided in the local rules. 

Comment: Negotiable instruments, trial exhibits, and trial notebooks are examples of items that are not to be filed in the court file either in paper form or by eFiling. 

## (3) **Documents That May Be eFiled:** 

- (A) Documents from governments or other courts under official seal including adoption documents. If filed electronically, the filing party must retain the original document during the pendency of any appeal and until at least sixty (60) days after completion of the instant case and shall present the original document to the court if requested to do so. This does not include documents that are or will be submitted as an exhibit in a hearing or trial. 

(4) **Waiver of the Requirement to eFile** . If an attorney is unable to eFile a document or documents, the attorney may request a waiver. The attorney must show good cause and explain why the attorney needs to file a paper document or documents in that particular case. The Clerk will make waiver request forms available, consider each application, and provide a written response to the attorney. The Clerk may make a waiver determination as to a document, or a series of documents, or for a particular case. Attorneys who receive an approved waiver shall file a copy of the waiver in each case that they file documents. Attorneys who have received a waiver shall place the words “Exempt from eFiling per waiver filed on (date)” in the caption of all paper documents they file for the duration of the waiver. 

(5) **Non-Compliance with this Rule** . If an attorney files a document in paper form and does not have an approved waiver from mandatory eFiling, the Clerk is authorized to reject the document and return it to the attorney for eFiling. 

(6) **Working Copies** . Pursuant to WCCR 10.2, judicial working copies may be submitted to the judicial officer or the judicial officer’s court designee using the eFiling system as authorized by local rule. 

9 

(7) **Voluminous Documents** - There is not a page limit on efiling, however, there is a file size limit. The max file size per document is 25 MB. If the submitted document is larger than that, filers must split the document. For example, if filing a declaration with exhibits that make it too large or long, they could submit the declaration as one filing, and submit the exhibits as another filing or filings. Filers should split the documents at points that make sense. Since each submission must comply with the court rules, the exhibits should have a cover sheet that has the case number, case caption, and an indication of what’s attached (ex: Exhibits A-D to Declaration of James Brown). 

## **(c) Electronic Service** . 

(1) **Effecting eService** . When a party eFiles a document, the party may electronically serve (eServe) the document(s) via the eServe feature within the eFiling system. eService under this subsection constitutes service under CR 5 and is complete as stated in CR 5(b)(7). An affidavit of service is required to be eFiled unless the court has a local rule that allows an automated certificate of service to satisfy the requirement. The affidavit of service must include certification of which contacts were selected to be served within the eServe system. Exceptions: This subsection does not apply when a statute or rule requires that a document be personally served on the receiving party; or when the receiving party is not represented by an attorney and has not registered to accept eService. 

(2) **Accepting eService** . Attorneys may elect to accept eService via the eFiling system in each case in which the attorney appears by providing their email address designated for electronic service through the electronic filing system. Likewise, a party that is not represented by an attorney may register their email address to accept eService via the eFiling system in each case in which the party eFiles a document. 

(3) **Unrepresented Litigants.** Unrepresented litigants are not required to use eServe but may elect to accept eService by providing their email address on the “service contact list” in the eFiling system. 

(4) **Non-Compliance** . If an attorney or unrepresented litigant serves a document in a manner that does not comply with this rule, the document shall be deemed not served pursuant to CR 5, and the Court in its discretion can refuse to consider the document. 

(5) **Subject to modification** . This section is subject to modification by local court rule or order. 

[Effective 9/1/25] 

## **WCGR 31 - Personal Identifiers – Children.** 

- a) **Complete names of children in sealed case types** . The complete names of children shall be used in cases that are confidential pursuant to state or federal statutes, including cases 

10 

filed pursuant to Title 13 RCW (excluding offender cases); Chapter 4.24 RCW, Chapter 26.33 (Adoption) and Chapter 71.34 (Mental Health Services for Minors). 

- b) **Confidential Information Form** . The complete names of children and other identifiers shall be included in the Confidential Form or similar document for cases filed under Title 26. 

- c) **Domestic Relations Orders** . Court orders concerning the financial support or the custody or residential schedule of a child (including temporary and permanent parenting plans and similar documents) and orders establishing or disestablishing paternity shall include the full name of the child. The date of birth of a child shall be included in court records only as authorized by GR 22. 

- d) **Child who is alleged to be victim of a crime** . The complete name of a child who is alleged to be a victim of a crime may be included on subpoenas and in jury instructions. Nothing in this rule requires that subpoenas be filed in the court file. 

- e) **Child who is charged with a crime** . The complete name of a child charged with a crime shall be included in any indictment or information filed with the court pursuant to CrR 2.1 or JuCR 7.2, as part of an affidavit or declaration of probable cause or for any other purpose deemed necessary for the prosecution or defense of the criminal or juvenile offender matter. Juvenile offenders shall be referred to as “respondent.” 

- f) **Orders issued for the protection of or restricting access to a child** . If a child is protected by a criminal no contact order issued pursuant to 10.99 RCW, an antiharassment order issued pursuant to 10.14 RCW, an order of protection issued pursuant to 7.105 RCW, a restraining order or order of protection issued pursuant to 26.09 RCW, 26.10 RCW, 26.26 RCW, RCW 26.52.020, or any other court order entered for the protection of the child, the child’s full name and other identifiers shall be included on petitions and orders as necessary for entry of the order into the Judicial Information System (JIS) and/or the Washington Crime Information Center (WACIC). If access to a child is restricted pursuant to CrR 3.2 (d)(1), the court may include the full name of the child on the order, if deemed necessary for effective enforcement of the order. 

- g) **Orders restraining child from contacting or harassing others** . Whenever a child is named as a respondent in an order listed in (c) above, the child’s full name and other personal identifiers shall be included on the petition and order as necessary for entry of the order in the Judicial Information System (JIS) and./or the Washington Crime Information Center (WACIC). 

- h) **General Authority** . Nothing in this rule shall prohibit the court from authorizing or prohibiting the use of a child’s full name or date of birth when necessary for the safety of the child or for the orderly administration of justice, consistent with the requirements of GR 22.

[Effective 9/1/17, amended 9/1/25] 

## **WCGR 33 – Requests for Accommodations for Persons with Disabilities** 

- a) **Purpose** . It is the policy of the Whatcom County Superior Court to ensure that persons with disabilities have equitable and meaningful access to the court. Nothing in this rule shall be construed to limit or invalidate the remedies, rights and procedures accorded to any person under local, state, or federal law, including Washington Court Rules General Rule 33 (GR 33), the Americans with Disabilities Act of 1990 (42 U.S.C., secs. 12101-12213) (the ADA), the Washington Law Against Discrimination (RCW 49.60)(WLAD) or other local, state, or federal law. 

- b) **Procedure** . Requests for accommodation pursuant to GR 33 should be made by completing and submitting the form available on the court’s website either in person or by mail to the Superior Court Administrator, 311 Grand Avenue, Suite 500, Bellingham, WA 98225, or by emailing the form to WC_SuperiorCourtADARequests@co.whatcom.wa.us. 

- c) **Supporting documentation** . The court may request additional information about the qualifying disability to help assess the appropriate accommodation. Medical and other health information shall be submitted under the cover sheet available on the court’s website and such information shall be accessible only to the court and the person requesting the accommodation unless expressly ordered otherwise. 

- d) **Timing** . Requests should be made in advance whenever possible. 

- e) **Consideration.** The request will be considered by the Presiding Judge or their designee. 

- f) **Decision.** The court will inform the person requesting an accommodation of its decision, and a written decision will be filed in the court file related to the request, if any, or in the court’s administrative file.  The court may order that the decision be sealed. 

- g) **When Motion required** . When the accommodation requested is a continuance, stay, or adjustment of deadlines in a pending case, the person requesting the accommodation shall schedule the matter for a hearing, with notice to any opposing party. If such a matter comes before a judge without a hearing, the judge may require that the person making the request schedule a hearing. 

- [Effective 9/1/24]

## **WCCR 6 - Civil Motions (** _**formerly WCCR 77.2**_ **)** 

- a) **Court Calendar Schedule** . Motion hearings are scheduled on various commissioner calendars throughout the week. Judges hear special set motions and trials Monday through Thursday 8:30am – 4:30pm, and a Civil Law & Motion Calendar on Fridays at 1:30 pm. The Court Calendar Schedule is available on the Court’s website and should be consulted prior to scheduling a hearing. The Court Calendar Schedule may be modified from time to time (including one-time changes for holidays or judicial conferences) without formal republication. The Court Calendar Schedule details which matters require pre-arrangement, confirmation, or both. Parties should consult the court’s website for specific procedures for each judicial officer, including delivery of judge’s copies of documents. 

- b) **Special Set Hearings** . Parties may schedule special set hearings by contacting the judicial assistant for the assigned judicial officer. Special set hearings do not require confirmation, but must comply with the timelines and page limits of these rules. Parties should consider a Special Set on criminal and civil matters expected to last longer than 10 minutes. 

- c) **Friday Civil Law and Motions with Judges** . Civil motions not heard on a regular commissioner’s calendar may be scheduled for hearing on the assigned judge’s Friday civil law and motion calendar or special set with the assigned judge subject to compliance with WCCR 10.2 and 10.3 and the following provisions: 

   - 1) Parties must consult the judicial unavailability information posted on the court’s web page prior to scheduling a motion. Any motion scheduled for a date when a judge is unavailable will be stricken. 

   - 2) All motions must be confirmed no sooner than five (5) court days prior to the hearing and no later than noon two (2) court days prior to the hearing by calling or, preferably, emailing the assigned judge’s judicial assistant using the appropriate email confirmation link available on the court’s web page under “Confirming Your Hearing.” 

14 

Notice of confirmation must also be provided to all other parties. Motions which are not confirmed will be stricken from the calendar. 

- 3) Motions shall be filed with the clerk and served on all parties by noon nine (9) court days prior to the hearing. A proposed form of an order, which the court may adopt, modify or reject consistent with its decision shall be filed and served with the motion. Responses shall be filed and served on all parties by noon four (4) court days before hearing. Replies shall be filed and served on all parties no later than noon two (2) court days prior to the hearing. Parties should come to the motion calendar with original orders so that they may be entered without an additional hearing whenever possible. Judge’s copies of all documents must be provided to the judicial assistant at the time the original documents are filed with the clerk. Please consult the court’s website for instructions for providing judge’s copies, which may vary by Department. 

- 4) The filing deadlines of this rule do not apply to summary judgment motions, which are governed by CR 56. Summary judgment motions shall be served and filed pursuant to CR 56 and should comply with WCCR 10.2 and 10.3. 

- 5) If a party fails to appear in opposition to a motion, upon proof of proper service the court may grant the order requested unless the court shall deem it manifestly unauthorized. If no party appears, the motion may be deemed waived or stricken. No more than ten (10) minutes will be allowed to each side for argument unless the court otherwise directs. ## **WCCR 10.3 – Page Limits** 

- a) Absent prior authorization from the court, no individual document, whether titled as a motion, declaration, affidavit, memorandum or brief, shall exceed fifteen (15) pages, not including exhibits. No exhibits shall be considered unless referred to specifically in the underlying document. All such documents shall be double-spaced and typed in a font no smaller than 12 point. 

- b) Response to Motion. Absent prior authorization from the court, no response to any motion, declaration, affidavit, memorandum or brief shall exceed fifteen (15) pages, not including exhibits. No response may include a document or pleading already filed as part of the court record [see WCCR 10.2(h)], but instead shall cite to the relevant portion of the pertinent document. All such responses shall be double-spaced and typed in a font no smaller than 12 point. 

19 

- c) Reply to Response. Absent prior authorization from the court, no reply to a response to motion, declaration, affidavit, memorandum or brief shall exceed five (5) pages, not including exhibits. No reply may include a document or pleading already filed as part of the court record under the same cause number [see WCCR 10.2 (h)], but instead shall cite to the relevant portion of the pertinent document. All such replies shall be double-spaced and typed in a font no smaller than 12 point. 

- d) Family Law Declarations. Absent prior authorization from the court, declarations or affidavits in family law cases (whether initial, responsive, or reply declarations or affidavits) are limited to a combined total of fifteen (15) pages per party and no single declaration or affidavit may exceed five (5) pages. 

- e) Family Law Exhibits to Declarations. Absent prior authorization from the court, exhibits attached to declarations or affidavits of parties or non-expert witnesses shall count towards the fifteen-page limit in section (d) above, exclusive of any cover page numbering such exhibit. Exhibits shall be referenced in the declaration or affidavit, indicating the purpose for attaching them.  Copies of email, text, or other electronic communication attached as exhibits shall indicate the name of the sender and date sent. Exhibits consisting of multiple pages shall be numbered. No portion of this rule shall be construed to permit multiple motions to be noted for the same day to avoid the page limit. 

- f) Family Law Financial Declarations. Financial Declarations and financial documents in family law cases do not count toward the page limit. 

- g) Family Law Professional Reports and Evaluations. Declarations, affidavits, and reports from professionals such as guardians ad litem, child protection workers, law enforcement officers, parenting evaluators, substance abuse evaluators, mental health evaluators and providers, and other expert witnesses in family law cases do not count toward the page limit but should be properly authenticated and filed as separate documents, under seal if required. 

- h) Previously filed documents. Any document already filed as part of the court record is subject to the provisions of WCCR 10.2 (h). 

- i) Miscellaneous Exceptions. Deposition excerpts must be cited to by page and line number. They do not count toward the page limit. 

- j) Failure to Comply with Page Limits. If one or more parties violate the page limits set out above, the court may strike the pleadings, refuse to consider portions of the materials in violation, strike or continue the hearing and/or impose sanctions. 

20 

- k) Overlength Briefs: A party shall note for hearing any motion to file an overlength brief and include a declaration supporting why pages in excess of the requirements of this rule are necessary. If the parties agree, or as ordered by the court, oral argument may be waived. Leave of court is required before the overlength brief may be filed.

## **WCCR 17 – Unlawful Detainer/Eviction** 

Complaints for unlawful detainer, money judgments, and other orders in unlawful detainer cases, including writs of restitution, will be granted only under the following conditions: 

- a) Owners or lessors of real property, or any person properly designated as their representative, may properly be a plaintiff in unlawful detainer actions. Plaintiffs who are individual people may represent themselves if they choose not to be represented by a lawyer. Institutional plaintiffs (corporations, limited liability corporations, and partnerships) must be represented by an attorney licensed in Washington. 

- b) All complaints for unlawful detainer must include the following: 

   - 1) A copy of the rental agreement shall be filed with the complaint; 

   - 2) Plaintiff owners of the real property must state ownership in their complaint; 

   - 3) Plaintiff lessors and sublessors must state their status as lessor or sublessor in their complaint; 

   - 4) Representatives of property owners, including property managers, must state their status as representative in the complaint and must file with the complaint a copy of their written designation as representative; and 

   - 5) If the action is brought under the Residential Landlord Tenant Act and is based upon any reason other than nonpayment of rent exclusively, the plaintiff shall specifically plead the just cause exception under RCW 59.18 to ending a residential tenancy and shall state with specificity the facts supporting such exception. 

- c) The Court may order a writ of restitution to a property owner, lessor, sublessor, or representative of the property owner. A plaintiff seeking a writ of restitution must schedule a Show Cause Hearing on the issuance of the writ, with proper notice to the defendant of the hearing in accordance with WCCR 6, and notice that failure to attend may result in a default judgment and writ of restitution.  Notice of the hearing must be by an Order to Show Cause, which may be served with the summons and complaint or at any time thereafter. The Court 

21 

will not issue an order of default, or an Order for Writ of Restitution, until the hearing has occurred. A properly served defendant’s failure to appear at the hearing will be treated as default. Any motion to stay enforcement of the writ under RCW 59.18.410(3)(a) shall include proper service on the non-moving parties. 

- d) The court will grant a money judgment only when: 

   - 1) The plaintiff is the owner of the real property, or 

   - 2) The plaintiff is a lessor or sublessor and the rental agreement requires payments to be made directly to the lessor or sublessor, or 

   - 3) The plaintiff is a representative of the owner, the rental agreement requires payments to be made directly to the representative, and the appointment of the representative filed with the complaint authorizes the representative to collect judgments in their individual capacity.

## **GUARDIAN AD LITEM GRIEVANCE PROCEDURES (WCGAL)** 

## **WCGAL 7.1 - Guardian ad Litem Advisory Committee** 

The court’s Guardian ad Litem Advisory Committee, referred to as the "Committee," will administer complaints about Guardians ad Litem. 

[Effective 9/1/00; Revised 9/1/19] 

## **WCGAL 7.2 - Submission of Complaints** 

All complaints must be filed within six (6) months of the court’s discharge of the Guardian ad Litem. Complaints must be in writing and must be submitted to the Superior Court Administrator. All complaints must bear the signature, name and address of the person filing the complaint. 

[Effective 9/1/00; Revised 9/1/19] 

## **WCGAL 7.3 - Review of Complaint** 

Upon receipt of a written complaint, the Superior Court Administrator shall convene the Committee to review the complaint. The Superior Court Administrator may forward the complaint to an assigned judicial officer if proceedings in the GAL/CV involved case are ongoing. The Committee’s investigation begins on the date that the Notice of Complaint is sent to all Committee members, by any electronic or written means. The Chair of the Advisory Committee is the contact person for all parties and no discussion or other contact with other members of the Committee may be had by any party during the investigation except as specifically requested by the Advisory Committee Chair. Upon review of the complaint, the Committee shall either: 

- a) Make a finding that the complaint is with regard to a case then pending in the court and decline to review the complaint and so inform the complainant. In such instances the Committee shall advise the complainant that the complaint may only be addressed in the context of the case at bar, either by seeking the removal of the Guardian ad Litem or by contesting the information or recommendation contained in the Guardian ad Litem’s report or testimony. In such cases the Committee and its members shall perform its role in such a manner as to assure that the trial judge remains uninformed as to the complaint; or 

- b) Make a finding that the complaint has no merit on its face, and decline to review the complaint and so inform the complainant; or 

- c) Make a finding that the complaint appears to have merit and request a written response from the Guardian ad Litem within ten (10) business days, detailing the specific issues in the complaint to which the Committee desires a response. The Committee shall provide 

53 

the Guardian ad Litem with a copy of the original complaint. Once the Committee’s investigation begins, no additional documents or information will be considered unless specifically requested by the Committee. In considering whether the complaint has merit, the Committee shall consider whether the complaint alleges the Guardian ad Litem has: 

- 1) Violated a code of conduct; 

- 2) Misrepresented their qualifications to serve as a Guardian ad Litem; 

- 3) Breached the confidentiality of the parties; 

- 4) Falsified information in a report to the court or in testimony before the court; 

- 5) Failed, when required, to report abuse of a child; 

- 6) Communicated with a judicial officer _ex parte_ concerning a case for which they are serving as a Guardian ad Litem; 

- 7) Violated state or local laws or court rules; or 

- 8) Taken or failed to take any other action which would reasonably place the suitability of the Guardian ad Litem in question. 

[Effective 9/1/00; Revised 9/1/19, 9/1/25] 

## **WCGAL 7.4 Response and Findings** 

- a) Upon receipt of a written response to a complaint from the Guardian ad Litem, the Committee shall make a finding as to each of the specific issues in the complaint to which the Committee desires a response, as delineated in the Committee’s letter to the Guardian ad Litem. Such findings shall state that either there is no merit to the issue based upon the Guardian ad Litem’s response or that there is merit to the issue. 

- b) The Committee shall have the authority to issue a written admonishment, a written reprimand, refer the Guardian ad Litem to additional training, or recommend to the Presiding Judge that the court suspend or remove the Guardian ad Litem from the registry. In considering a response, the Committee shall take into consideration any prior complaints that resulted in an admonishment, reprimand, referral to training, or suspension or removal from a registry. If a Guardian ad Litem is listed on more than one registry, the suspension or removal may apply to each registry the Guardian ad Litem is listed on, at the discretion of the Committee. 

- c) The complainant and the Guardian ad Litem shall be notified in writing of the Committee’s decision following receipt of the Guardian ad Litem’s response. 

[Effective 9/1/00]

## **SUPERIOR COURT CIVIL ARBITRATION RULES (WCSCCAR)** 

## **WCSSCAR 1.1 - Application of Rules – Purpose and Definition** 

- a) **Purpose** . The Whatcom County Superior Court Civil Arbitration Rules (WCSCCAR) implement RCW 7.06 and the Superior Court Civil Arbitration Rules adopted by the Washington State Supreme Court (SCCAR). Arbitration provides a simplified and economical procedure for obtaining prompt and equitable resolution of disputes. The SCCAR, as supplemented by these rules, are not designed to address every question that may arise during the arbitration process, and the rules give considerable discretion to the arbitrator. The arbitrator should not hesitate to exercise that discretion. Arbitration hearings should be informal and expeditious, consistent with the purpose of the relevant statutes and rules. 

- b) **“Director” defined** . “Director” means the Director of Arbitration for Whatcom County Superior Court. The appointment of the director and other administrative matters are addressed in WCSSCAR 8.6. 

- c) **Limits** . The amount in controversy may be up to one hundred thousand dollars ($100,000), exclusive of attorney fees, interest, and costs as authorized by RCW 7.06.020. 

- d) **Discovery** . Any discovery pending when the case is assigned to the arbitrator is stayed unless the arbitrator orders otherwise. Discovery concerns may be resolved by the appointed arbitrator, who is authorized to order discovery as reasonably necessary. 

[Effective 9/16/05, amended 9/1/19, 9/1/22, 9/1/24] 

## **WCSSCAR 1.2 – Matters Subject to Mandatory Superior Court Civil Arbitration** 

Any civil action filed in Whatcom County Superior Court, other than an appeal from a court of limited jurisdiction, is subject to arbitration under these rules if the sole relief sought is a money judgment (a) in which no party asserts a claim in excess of the amount authorized by RCW 7.06.020, exclusive of attorney fees, interest, and costs, or (b) in which all parties for purposes of arbitration waive claims in excess of that amount. Parties are encouraged to stipulate to the arbitration of any matter in controversy. See WCSSCAR 8.1. 

[Effective 6/1/91, amended 9/1/19, 9/1/22, 9/1/24]

All motions before the Court relating to arbitrability or the assignment of an arbitrator shall be scheduled on the civil motions calendar in accordance with WCCR 6, except as otherwise provided in these rules. Once cases have been transferred to arbitration, all motions shall be heard by the arbitrator. ## **WCSSCAR 2.1 – Transfer to Arbitration and Assignment of Arbitrator**

   - 3) By order of the court on the merits.

## **WCSSCAR 2.3 – Assignment of Arbitrator** 

- a) **Stipulations** . The parties are encouraged to stipulate to an arbitrator. A master list of arbitrators will be furnished upon request. In the absence of a stipulation, a list of five (5) arbitrators will be provided to the parties and the arbitrator will be chosen from the list as described in this rule. 

- b) **Response by Parties** . Each party may, within fourteen (14) calendar days after the list of proposed arbitrators is delivered to the parties, nominate one (1) or two (2) arbitrators and strike two (2) arbitrators from the list. If both parties respond, an arbitrator nominated by both parties will be appointed. If no arbitrator has been nominated by both parties, the director will appoint an arbitrator from among those not stricken by either party. 

- c) **Response by only one party** . If only one party responds within fourteen (14) calendar days, the director will appoint one (1) of the five (5) proposed arbitrators. 

- d) **No Response** . If neither party responds within fourteen (14) calendar days, the director will appoint one of the five proposed arbitrators. 

- e) **Additional arbitrators for additional parties** . If there are more than two (2) adverse parties, such parties may request the director to include additional proposed arbitrators on the list, with the above principles of selection to be applied. The number of adverse parties

## **WCSSCAR 3.1 – Arbitrators** 

## **a) Qualifications.** 

   - 1) An arbitrator must be a member of the Washington State Bar Association who has been admitted to the Bar for at least five (5) years and who is a current member of the Whatcom County Bar Association, or who is a retired judicial officer. By stipulation, the parties to a case may waive this requirement. 

   - 2) Unless waived pursuant to RCW 7.06.040 (2)(b), a person may not serve as an arbitrator unless the person has completed a minimum of three credits of Washington State Bar Association-approved continuing legal education credits on the professional and ethical considerations for serving as an arbitrator. As part of a person’s application to be included on the panel of arbitrators, the person shall submit a declaration or affidavit that they are in compliance with the qualifications described in RCW 7.06.040. 

- b) **Arbitration Panel** . There shall be a panel of arbitrators in such numbers as the Administrative Committee may from time to time determine pursuant to WCSSCAR 8.6. A person desiring to serve as an arbitrator shall complete an application on the form prescribed by the court. A list showing the names of arbitrators available to hear cases and their applications shall be available for public inspection in the director’s office. The oath of office on the form prescribed by the court must be completed prior to an applicant being placed on the panel. The director shall perform an annual review to determine whether current arbitrators are willing and able to continue to serve on the panel and present those findings to the Arbitration Committee for further action. 

- c) **Refusal; Disqualification** . The appointment of an arbitrator is subject to the right of that person to refuse to serve. An arbitrator should notify the director if their impartiality might reasonably be questioned. Counsel knowing any facts that would raise a reasonable question about the impartiality of the assigned arbitrator shall notify the arbitrator and the director. In the absence of such notice, the grounds therefore are deemed waived. If disqualified, the arbitrator must immediately return all case materials to the director.

## **WCSSCAR 3.2 - Authority and Responsibility of Arbitrators** 

An arbitrator has the authority and responsibility to: 

- a) Conduct the arbitration in an equitable and impartial manner, in accordance with applicable statutes and rules, and to make awards; 

- b) Determine the time, place, and procedure to present a motion before the arbitrator; 

- c) Require a party or attorney or both to pay the reasonable expenses, including attorney fees, caused by the failure of such party or attorney, or both, to obey an order of the arbitrator, unless the arbitrator finds that the failure was substantially justified or that other circumstances make an award of expenses unjust. The arbitrator shall make a special award for such expenses and shall file such award with the Superior Court Clerk, with proof of service on each party. The aggrieved party shall have ten (10) calendar days thereafter to appeal the award of such expenses in accordance with the procedures set forth in RCW 2.24.050. If no party appeals the award within ten (10) calendar days after filing, a judgment shall be entered in a manner described generally under SCCAR 6.3. 

- d) Award attorney fees as authorized by these rules, by contract or by law, as if the matter were tried to a court; and 

- e) Issue subpoenas in accordance with SCCAR 4.3.

## **WCJuCR 1.4 – Applicability of other Rules** 

- a) **Civil Rules.** Whatcom County Superior Court Civil Rules (WCCR), including the provisions of WCCR 6(c), WCCR 10.2 and WCCR 10.3 shall apply in all juvenile proceedings other than those involving a juvenile offense when not inconsistent with these rules and applicable statutes. 

- b) **Criminal Rules.** The Whatcom County Superior Court Criminal Rules (WCCrR) shall apply to juvenile offense proceedings when not inconsistent with these rules and applicable statutes.

## **WCJuCR 2.4 – Juvenile Dependency/Termination - Appointment of GAL** 

When a Guardian ad Litem (GAL) is appointed pursuant to RCW 13.34, the court will first seek a volunteer from the court’s CASA program, which is managed by judicial (GR 29) employees. If a volunteer is not available for appointment, the court will appoint a suitable person, who may be a judicial employee. The court does not have a “Guardian ad litem program” as that term is defined in RCW 13.34.030 (13). 

[Effective 9/1/24] 

## **WCJuCR 6.0 – Referral to Diversion and Filing in Offender Matters** 

Consistent with the purposes of RCW 13.40.010, the court encourages the prompt referral and resolution of juvenile offense matters at all stages of the proceedings and discourages delays. 

- a) **Referrals to diversion.** The prosecuting attorney shall refer appropriate misdemeanor, gross misdemeanor and Class C felony matters to diversion promptly after receiving law enforcement reports. 

- b) **Filing of Information.** If, in the prosecuting attorney’s discretion and subject to RCW 13.40.070, charges are to be filed, the prosecuting attorney shall file charges promptly after receiving the complete law enforcement reports. Failure to promptly file charges may be grounds for dismissal of the charges. 

- c) **Motions to dismiss for delay.** The court will consider a motion to dismiss an Information based on a claim of unreasonable delay in filing which results in prejudice to the accused. 

71 

Unreasonable delay shall constitute an affirmative defense that must be raised by motion not later than five (5) court days after arraignment. Such motion may be considered by affidavit. If a prima facie showing of unreasonable delay is established, the court will determine whether or not dismissal or other sanctions will be imposed. The court will consider the following factors, and any other factors that relate to the issues: 

- 1) The length of the delay; 

- 2) the reason for the delay; 

- 3) The impact of the delay on the ability to defend against the charge; and 

- 4) The seriousness of the alleged offense.

      - 6) The sophistication and maturity of the juvenile as determined by consideration of the juvenile’s home, environmental situation, emotional attitude and pattern of living; 

      - 7) The record and previous history of the juvenile, including previous contacts with law enforcement, courts, probation/community supervision and prior commitments to juvenile institutions; 

      - 8) The prospects for adequate protection of the public and the likelihood of reasonable rehabilitation through services and programs available in juvenile court, if convicted of the offense; and 

      - 9) A recommendation to the court on the motion. 

   - c) The author of the declination investigation report shall be present at the hearing to testify if requested to do so by either party. 

- [Effective 9/1/06, amended 9/1/24] 

## **WCJuCR 10.2 – Recording Juvenile Court Proceedings** 

All proceedings shall be recorded unless waived pursuant to statute. The electronic recording device approved by the court is approved for all hearings and for all purposes. 

No others may record proceedings in juvenile court without prior court approval.
